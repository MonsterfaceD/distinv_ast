\section{Automatic Template-Based Certificate Synthesis}
\label{sec:certificate-synthesis}

We automatically search for AST certificates for probabilistic while
loops with loop-free bodies. Numerical state components are integer-valued;
guards and tests use linear arithmetic, and updates are piecewise affine.
Boolean state components may record finite control information.
The implementation takes a supplied encoding of the distribution produced
by one complete body execution, with finitely many outcomes and fixed
rational probabilities. It searches for \emph{guarded affine}
certificates with integer coefficients, or \emph{guarded piecewise affine}
certificates over a supplied finite partition. Both classes assign zero
to states in which the loop guard is false.

The caller provides the body encoding and an invariant; the procedure
constructs templates of the chosen shape, searches for their coefficients,
and verifies the resulting certificates over the entire invariant domain.
The search uses counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS): a learner
proposes coefficients, and a verifier either validates them or returns a
state that rules them out. This procedure is independent of the particular
loop being analyzed. The random walk, FDR, and FLDR below are instances
of the same input interface and synthesis algorithm, using both exact
and abstract descriptions of complete body executions.

\subsection{Loop Models, Templates, and Verification Conditions}
\label{sec:synthesis-constraints}

\paragraph{Supplied loop model.}
The input consists of a finite tuple of integer and Boolean state
components, a loop guard $b$, an initial-state description, an invariant
predicate $S$, and the complete body outcomes. The initial-state
description may include an initializer, such as $(n,1,0)$ for FDR under
the input assumption $n\geq1$.
An exact body encoding gives update functions $T_1,\ldots,T_h$ and
fixed positive rational weights $p_1,\ldots,p_h$ with $\sum_jp_j=1$,
such that, at each enabled state in $S$,
\[
 \den{B}(\dirac{\sigma})
 =\sum_{j=1}^{h}p_j\,\dirac{T_j(\sigma)}.
\]
Each $T_j$ describes the final state after all assignments, tests, and
choices of an iteration, including any reset.
The supported encodings use Boolean combinations of linear integer
arithmetic and integer-valued piecewise affine updates. They are supplied
as Python/Z3 expressions; the implementation does not parse source
programs or derive their semantics or invariants.

Read-only program parameters are state components that the updates leave
unchanged. For example, the FDR input $n$ remains unchanged during a run,
but can range over all positive integers in one synthesis problem.
These parameters are distinct from the unknown coefficients of a
certificate template. A fixed numerical input, such as the FLDR weights
$(4,5)$, is substituted into the model; an unspecified integer input
remains a symbolic state component.

\paragraph{Certificate templates.}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ be all declared integer components, including
symbolic read-only inputs, and write $x_i(\sigma)=\sigma(x_i)$ for
their values. The automatically constructed affine basis is
$(x_1,\ldots,x_d,1)$. Boolean flags may occur in the guard and in
partition predicates, but are not numerical basis elements.
For the supermartingale $v$ and variant $u$ of \Cref{thm:program-rule},
the single-region templates are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_{\mathbf a}(\sigma)&=[b](\sigma)
   \left(a_0+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_i x_i(\sigma)\right),\\
 U_{\mathbf u}(\sigma)&=[b](\sigma)
   \left(u_0+\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_i x_i(\sigma)\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here $[b](\sigma)$ is $1$ when the loop is enabled and $0$ otherwise.
The integers in $\mathbf a=(a_0,\ldots,a_d)$ and
$\mathbf u=(u_0,\ldots,u_d)$ are the unknown template coefficients:
one coefficient vector defines a function for all states and all
admitted values of the symbolic inputs. The two vectors are independent
unknowns, although the constraints below couple the resulting functions.
We use $V,U$ for these program-level certificates in this section;
they instantiate $v,u$ and carry no control-location argument.

For example, the FDR variant template is
$[v<n](u_0+u_n n+u_v v+u_c c)$. The procedure searches for the
four coefficients; the program input $n$ remains universally quantified.
The active expression $n-v+c$ is obtained by setting
$(u_0,u_n,u_v,u_c)=(0,1,-1,1)$.

\paragraph{Verification conditions.}
For a proposed pair $V,U$, require the following at every
$\sigma\models S\land b$:
\begin{gather}
 V(\sigma)\geq1,\qquad U(\sigma)\geq1,
 \label{eq:syn-positive}\\
 \sum_{j=1}^{h}p_j V(T_j(\sigma))\leq V(\sigma),
 \label{eq:syn-supermartingale}\\
 \bigvee_{j=1}^{h}\bigl(U(T_j(\sigma))\leq U(\sigma)-1\bigr),
 \label{eq:syn-progress}\\
 U(\sigma)\leq V(\sigma).
 \label{eq:syn-domination}
\end{gather}
The first line enforces positivity while the loop is enabled.
The second is the supermartingale inequality for a complete iteration.
The third requires at least one outcome to decrease the natural-valued
variant; that outcome has probability at least
$\varepsilon=\min_jp_j>0$. This constant is computed from the supplied
weights, rather than searched for. The final inequality supplies a simple
sufficient condition for sublevel boundedness: $V\leq r$ implies
$U\leq\lfloor r\rfloor$.
Every experiment below has two fair-bit outcomes, denoted $T_0,T_1$.
There the expectation condition becomes
$V(T_0(\sigma))+V(T_1(\sigma))\leq2V(\sigma)$ and
$\varepsilon=1/2$.
Requiring $U\leq V$ and integer-coefficient templates restricts the
search beyond the general rule, which permits real-valued
supermartingales and more general sublevel bounds.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the synthesis constraints]
\label{prop:synthesis-soundness}
Suppose $S$ contains the initial loop-head states and is preserved by
every outcome of an exact body encoding from enabled states.
Every template instantiation
satisfying \eqref{eq:syn-positive}--\eqref{eq:syn-domination} supplies
certificates for \Cref{thm:program-rule} with
$\varepsilon=\min_jp_j$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $I=\{\mu\in\subdists\mid\supp(\mu)\subseteq S\}$, which is
a distributional invariant by the assumptions on $S$.
The guarded templates vanish on terminal states; on enabled states,
$V$ is positive and $U$ is a positive integer. The weighted inequality
is the required expectation bound. At least one outcome of weight
at least $\min_jp_j$ decreases $U$, and $U\leq V$ bounds $U$ by
$\lfloor r\rfloor$ on each $V$-sublevel. Thus all premises hold.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Supplied abstractions of the body.}
When a concrete update depends on a data structure, the caller may
instead supply an overapproximation of its complete outcomes.
Auxiliary variables $\xi$ describe which updates are possible, and
a formula $A(\sigma,\xi)$ selects the allowed descriptions.
For each allowed $\xi$, the functions $T_j(\sigma,\xi)$ and weights
$p_j$ define one candidate body distribution. We require the
verification conditions for every allowed description.
The variables $\xi$ describe transitions; they are neither unknown
template coefficients nor additional numerical certificate features.
For example, the FLDR abstraction uses two integers to say whether
each bit continues, resets, or accepts.

The caller must justify that every concrete body distribution is
represented by an allowed description under the chosen representation
of states. Then the same soundness argument applies, since the
conditions hold for every concrete transition.
The implementation checks that every enabled state admits at least one
description and that all described successors preserve $S$.
These checks prevent an empty or non-inductive abstract model; the
connection to the concrete program requires the separate argument
given for FLDR below.

\subsection{Counterexample-Guided Search and Fixed Partitions}
\label{sec:synthesis-cegis}

Before searching for coefficients, the implementation checks that the
supplied initialization establishes $S$ and that every represented
outcome preserves $S$ from an enabled state. If an abstraction is used,
it also checks
$S\land b\Rightarrow\exists\xi\,A(\sigma,\xi)$.
This last query may contain quantification. An SMT solver, here Z3,
is used to decide the resulting logical constraints.

Fix a coefficient bound $B$. Each unknown coefficient is an integer
between $-B$ and $B$. The search proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \emph{Choose a sample.} The solver selects an enabled state
 from $S$, together with an admissible description $\xi$ when needed.
 This is a test point for certificate search. If no enabled state
 exists in $S$, the loop terminates immediately on its initial states.
 \item \emph{Propose coefficients.} The learner chooses both vectors
 so that all verification conditions hold on the accumulated samples.
 It minimizes the sum of absolute coefficient values to favor small
 expressions; this objective has no soundness role.
 \item \emph{Check all states.} With these coefficients fixed, the
 verifier asks whether any enabled state in $S$ violates a condition.
 For an abstract encoding it also searches over all admissible $\xi$.
 A satisfying assignment supplies a counterexample state and, if
 applicable, its transition description. These are added to the
 samples before returning to the learner.
 \item \emph{Accept a verified pair.} If the verifier query is
 unsatisfiable, all conditions hold over the entire invariant domain.
 This includes all admitted values of unbounded integer inputs.
 The implementation then checks the conditions once more individually.
\end{enumerate}
The learner and verifier alternate which quantities are fixed.
On a fixed sample, the constraints are linear in the unknown template
coefficients. With coefficients fixed, they are Boolean combinations
of linear arithmetic in the state and auxiliary transition variables
for the supported encodings. The verification domain is never bounded
by the coefficient bound $B$.

Each counterexample excludes at least the current candidate and no
valid candidate. Therefore, with exact terminating solvers and no
resource limits, the search terminates for each fixed finite set of
coefficient choices: it either returns a verified pair or establishes
that no pair in that set satisfies these sufficient conditions.
The implementation tries $B=1,2,4,8,16$, retaining samples between
bounds. An unsatisfiable learner query excludes the current bound;
the next bound is then tried. Exhausting the schedule excludes only
the tested templates. Unknown solver results, timeouts, and the limit
of 500 proposed candidates are inconclusive.
Relative completeness of the proof rule does not imply completeness
of this restricted synthesis procedure.

\paragraph{Fixed piecewise affine templates.}
\label{sec:synthesis-piecewise}
The same procedure accepts a supplied finite partition
$P_1,\ldots,P_q$ of the enabled states in $S$.
It checks that the regions cover this domain and are pairwise disjoint,
then creates a separate affine coefficient block for each region and
each certificate. For example, the supermartingale template is
\[
 V(\sigma)=[b](\sigma)\sum_{j=1}^{q}[P_j](\sigma)
 \left(a_{j,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{j,i}x_i(\sigma)\right).
\]
The variant template has its own coefficient blocks.
The partition predicates are supplied Boolean combinations of linear
arithmetic over declared state components, including symbolic program
inputs. They cannot depend on template coefficients or auxiliary
transition variables. The verifier evaluates the partition again at
each successor, so it also checks transitions between regions.
The verification conditions and their soundness argument are unchanged.

For a threshold split, the caller supplies both predicates, for example
$x<0$ and $x\geq0$, or $c<k-1$ and $c\geq k-1$.
In the latter case $k$ is a read-only program input, so the split is
fixed as an expression even though its numerical position depends on
that input. The algorithm searches for coefficients within the regions;
it does not search for the partition or its thresholds.
Additional regions can express certificates unavailable to a single
affine template, but also enlarge the finite search space.

\paragraph{Unbounded symmetric random walk.}
For \Cref{sec:walk}, the supplied model has $S\equiv x\geq0$, guard
$x>0$, and the equally likely updates $x\mapsto x-1$ and $x\mapsto x+1$.
The engine constructs the basis $(x,1)$ and finds $V=U=x$ on enabled
states, verified without an upper bound on $x$. The expectation is
unchanged, while the left outcome decreases $U$ by one.
This establishes AST despite infinite expected runtime:
for $0<x<N$, the expected time $x(N-x)$ to hit $\{0,N\}$ lower-bounds
the time to hit $0$, and diverges with $N$.
A uniform negative expected drift is unnecessary.

\paragraph{FDR with a symbolic range.}
For the loop of \Cref{sec:fdr}, the supplied invariant is
$S\equiv n\geq1\land1\leq v<2n\land0\leq c<v\land c<n$, and the
complete bit-$\beta$ update is
\[
 T_\beta(n,v,c)=
 \begin{cases}
 (n,2v-n,2c+\beta-n),&2c+\beta\geq n,\\
 (n,2v,2c+\beta),&2c+\beta<n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here $n$ is a read-only program input. From the declared integer
components the engine constructs the full basis $(n,v,c,1)$ and finds
$V=n$, $U=n-v+c$ on $v<n$, verified for all admitted values of $n$.
Put $\Delta=v-c$. Enabled states satisfy
$1\leq\Delta\leq n-1$ and $U=n-\Delta$. On bit zero a continuing
successor has $\Delta'=2\Delta$, giving $U'\leq U-1$; acceptance
sets $U'=0$. Since $n$ is unchanged, $V'$ is $n$ or zero, and $U\leq V$.
Thus synthesis recovers the affine certificates used in \Cref{thm:fdr}.
This variant also has a stronger property: on bit one,
$\Delta'=2\Delta-1\geq\Delta$ when continuing, so in fact
$\mathbb E[U']\leq U-1/2$. This additional inequality is independently
SMT-verified; $U$ itself is a ranking supermartingale here.

\paragraph{FLDR with fixed weights.}
For $(4,5)$, preprocessing gives $m=9$, $k=4$, and rejection weight $7$.
The artifact constructs the exact finite tree from binary weight columns,
placing internal nodes before leaves and leaf labels in descending order,
as in \Cref{sec:fldr}. The supplied invariant is its set of loop-head nodes,
the guard identifies internal nodes, and the two updates follow the
corresponding bit edges, including resets. The preprocessing quantities
$m,k,n$ are fixed numerical values in this model, leaving $c,d$ as the
integer state components. The engine therefore constructs the basis
$(c,d,1)$. After excluding $B=1$, it finds
$V=U=c-2d+2$ at $B=2$. \Cref{fig:fldr-synthesis-tree} displays the
five active nodes, acceptances, and resets. An independent rational-arithmetic
audit checks all nodes and edges (\Cref{app:artifact}). At the root the
expectation is unchanged, so this shared certificate has no uniformly
negative expected change.

\paragraph{FLDR with symbolic inputs and abstract outcomes.}
To cover arbitrary FLDR weight vectors without constructing every tree,
we supply an abstraction of the body outcomes. Its integer components
are $(m,k,n,c,d)$, with read-only inputs $m,k,n$; a Boolean flag
$\mathit{active}$ is the guard. The supplied invariant requires
$m\geq1$, $0\leq k\leq m$, $1\leq n\leq m$, $0\leq c\leq k$,
$d\geq0$, and $\mathit{active}\Rightarrow c<k$.
The auxiliary transition description is $\xi=(t_0,t_1)$, where each
$t_\beta\in\{0,1,2\}$ classifies the outcome of bit $\beta$ as
continuation, rejection, or acceptance, respectively.
Continuation advances to $(c+1,2d+\beta)$ and requires $c+1<k$;
rejection resets to $(0,0)$; acceptance advances to the child and
disables the guard. Continuation and rejection leave the guard enabled.
The inputs $m,k,n$ are unchanged. The relation $A(\sigma,\xi)$ excludes
$(t_0,t_1)=(1,1)$, so at least one outcome does not reject.

Tree height at most $k$ and at most one rejecting leaf per level justify
this abstraction of concrete FLDR executions (\Cref{sec:fldr}).
The certificate solver does not derive these facts from preprocessing.
The linear domain relaxes $k=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ to $0\leq k\leq m$,
avoiding logarithmic arithmetic. From all integer components, the engine
constructs the basis $(m,k,n,c,d,1)$ and finds $V=m$, $U=k-c$ on active
states. In this result, the coefficients of $n,d$ and the constant term
are zero in both certificates; $V$ uses only $m$, and $U$ only $k,c$.
Every successor has $V'$ equal to $m$ or zero, a nonrejecting outcome
decreases $U$ by at least one, and $1\leq U=k-c\leq k\leq m=V$.
The alternative pair $V=k$, $U=k-c$ is also independently verified,
but is not the candidate selected in the recorded affine run.
Verification ranges over every outcome pair admitted by $A$, including
pairs not realized by a particular concrete tree. The fixed-weight model
above supplies only that tree's exact transitions, so its different
certificate is consistent with this result.

The depth variant $U=k-c$ can grow in expectation: at $(c,d)=(2,0)$
in the fixed tree, $U=2$ but $\mathbb E[U']=5/2$.
The separate supermartingale still satisfies the expectation bound.
If we restrict the basis to $(k,c,1)$, omitting the node index $d$,
a shared function on enabled states at $k=4$ has the form
$W(c)=A+Dc$. Progress at the root
forces $D<0$, while its expected change at $(2,0)$ is then $-D/2>0$.
However, the synthesized $c-2d+2$ removes this obstruction. This separation
concerns a specified template class, not an intrinsic need for distinct
functions in FLDR.

\paragraph{What the supplied partition adds.}
For the symmetric walk on all of $\mathbb Z$, the supplied model has
guard $x\neq0$, invariant $S\equiv\mathit{true}$, and the two equally
likely updates $x\mapsto x-1$ and $x\mapsto x+1$.
An affine variant $ax+b$ positive on both unbounded
tails must have $a=0$, and a constant cannot decrease at $x=2$. Hence no
single-affine pair satisfies the constraints, regardless of its coefficient
bound. With the supplied split $x<0$ versus $x\geq0$, the same engine
synthesizes the expressions $-x$ and $x$ for the respective regions in
both templates, giving the universally verified pair $V=U=|x|$.
The partition is supplied; the affine expressions are found by
coefficient search. This demonstrates what a piecewise affine template
adds over the full invariant. Optional sampler partitions and
checks of cross-region transitions are documented in \Cref{app:artifact}.

\paragraph{Scope of the automation.}
For the supported loop models, synthesis automatically constructs the
chosen affine or piecewise affine templates, searches for their
coefficients, and verifies them over unbounded states and symbolic inputs.
Body encodings, invariants, abstraction arguments, and partitions remain
supplied inputs. The finite coefficient schedule, template shapes, and
$U\leq V$ condition limit which certificates can be found.
The experiments demonstrate feasibility; they establish neither
unrestricted synthesis completeness nor a performance advantage over
existing synthesis tools.
