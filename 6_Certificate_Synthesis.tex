\section{Template-Based Certificate Synthesis}
\label{sec:certificate-synthesis}

The iteration-level obligations of \Cref{thm:program-rule} also support
certificate synthesis. We instantiate them with a common
counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS) kernel for the random
walk, FDR, and FLDR. The input consists of declared state variables and
parameters, an initial-state predicate, an inductive state predicate, and
an exact or soundly overapproximated one-iteration semantics. The kernel
constructs affine templates automatically and searches for their
coefficients. The experiments establish feasibility of this restricted
instantiation of the rule; they do not introduce a new general-purpose
synthesis technique.

\subsection{Automatic Affine Templates and Sound Constraints}
\label{sec:synthesis-constraints}

For this section, numerical state variables and symbolic parameters are
integer-valued. Write $\mathbf{x}(\sigma)=(x_1(\sigma),\ldots,x_d(\sigma))$
for all declared integer state components, including immutable symbolic
parameters. Boolean flags may occur in the guard $b$ but are not numerical
features. Given a supplied predicate $S$ containing the initial states and
preserved by the body, the kernel constructs independent templates
\[
 V_{\mathbf a}(\sigma)=[b](\sigma)
   \Bigl(a_0+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_i x_i(\sigma)\Bigr),\qquad
 U_{\mathbf u}(\sigma)=[b](\sigma)
   \Bigl(u_0+\sum_{i=1}^{d}u_i x_i(\sigma)\Bigr).
\]
All coefficients are unknown integers. Thus no example-specific feature
subset or relationship between the two coefficient vectors is supplied.
The fixed hypothesis class is nevertheless restricted: functions are
affine on active states and zero outside the guard. A fixed numerical
input is substituted into the semantics, whereas an input that should
remain symbolic is declared as a parameter and included in the templates.

The presented examples use two equiprobable outcomes. For an exact input,
$T_0,T_1$ describe complete body executions, including deterministic
branches. An abstract input additionally supplies auxiliary descriptors
$\xi$ and a relation $A(\sigma,\xi)$ selecting admissible outcome pairs
$T_0(\sigma,\xi),T_1(\sigma,\xi)$. Every enabled state must admit a
pair, and every concrete outcome pair must be represented. The latter is
a required soundness argument for the supplied abstraction. Auxiliary
descriptors are not program-state features. Below we suppress $\xi$;
for abstract inputs, each condition is required for all admissible pairs.

For every state satisfying $S\land b$, impose
\begin{align}
 V(\sigma)&\geq1,& U(\sigma)&\geq1,
 \label{eq:syn-positive}\\
 V(T_0(\sigma))+V(T_1(\sigma))&\leq2V(\sigma),
 \label{eq:syn-supermartingale}\\
 U(T_0(\sigma))\leq U(\sigma)-1
 &\quad\lor\quad U(T_1(\sigma))\leq U(\sigma)-1,
 \label{eq:syn-progress}\\
 U(\sigma)&\leq V(\sigma).
 \label{eq:syn-domination}
\end{align}
The domination condition is sufficient for sublevel boundedness and is
stronger than the corresponding premise of \Cref{thm:program-rule}.
Integer coefficients and $V\geq1$ further restrict the search space.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the synthesis constraints]
\label{prop:synthesis-soundness}
Suppose $S$ contains the initial states and is preserved by every body
outcome. If an abstract input is used, suppose it represents every
concrete outcome pair. Any instantiation satisfying
\eqref{eq:syn-positive}--\eqref{eq:syn-domination} on $S\land b$,
for every admissible pair, supplies an AST certificate with
$\varepsilon=1/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The templates vanish on terminal states. On active states, $V$ is positive
and $U$ is a positive integer. The expectation inequality holds for each
concrete body distribution; for an abstract input this follows by selecting
its represented concrete pair. At least one outcome decreases $U$ by at
least one, giving progress probability at least $1/2$. On $V\leq r$, the
domination condition bounds $U$ by $\lfloor r\rfloor$. Finally,
subdistributions supported in $S$ form a distributional invariant for the
allowed inputs. The premises of \Cref{thm:program-rule} follow.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counterexample-Guided Search and Coefficient Bounds}
\label{sec:synthesis-cegis}

For a fixed $B\in\mathbb{N}$, each coefficient ranges over
$\{-B,\ldots,B\}$. An SMT query first selects an enabled sample
automatically. The learner searches simultaneously for both coefficient
vectors satisfying all obligations on the accumulated samples, minimizing
$\sum_i|a_i|+\sum_i|u_i|$. This objective prefers small expressions but
has no role in soundness; ties are left to the solver.

For a candidate with fixed coefficients, the verifier asks for a state
satisfying $S\land b$ that violates at least one obligation. For abstract
inputs, it also chooses an admissible descriptor $\xi$. A satisfying
assignment is added to the samples. An unsatisfiable counterexample query
establishes the obligations over the entire invariant domain; it is not
merely a bounded-state test. In contrast, an unsatisfiable learner query
excludes certificates only within the current coefficient range and the
chosen template class.

We restrict the supplied predicates and outcome descriptions to Boolean
combinations of linear arithmetic and piecewise affine updates. With a
sample fixed, the learner's constraints are linear in the coefficients;
with the coefficients fixed, the verifier's constraints are linear in the
state and auxiliary variables. The implementation therefore avoids a
single existential-universal query with unknown products of coefficients
and state variables. It checks initialization and preservation of $S$
separately. For an abstract input it also checks coverage,
$S\land b\Rightarrow\exists\xi\,A$; this input check may use
quantification, unlike the CEGIS certificate queries. Coverage does not
replace the mathematical argument that concrete FLDR transitions are
represented by the abstraction.

For fixed $B$, every counterexample permanently excludes at least the
current candidate, while preserving all valid candidates. With exact,
terminating solvers and no resource limits, the finite search therefore
terminates and is complete for the bounded template and the imposed
constraints. The implementation tries $B=1,2,4,8,16$, retaining samples
when an infeasible range is enlarged. Unknown results, timeouts, and the
candidate limit are inconclusive. The finite schedule does not establish
completeness for unrestricted affine certificates or for AST programs.

\subsection{User-Supplied Piecewise Affine Templates}
\label{sec:synthesis-piecewise}

The same kernel optionally accepts a finite partition of active invariant
states by fixed predicates $P_1,\ldots,P_q$. Each predicate is a Boolean
combination of linear arithmetic over declared state components; transition
descriptors cannot occur in it. The kernel checks coverage and pairwise
disjointness on $S\land b$. It then assigns an independent full affine
coefficient block to each region and each certificate, for example
\[
 V(\sigma)=[b](\sigma)\sum_{j=1}^{q}[P_j](\sigma)
   \left(a_{j,0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{j,i}x_i(\sigma)\right),
\]
and analogously for $U$ with independent coefficients. With one region
$P_1=\mathit{true}$, this is the original affine template.

A threshold split supplies $P_1\equiv e<r$ and $P_2\equiv e\geq r$.
The user fixes the expressions $e,r$; a threshold may be a numeral or a
linear expression in symbolic parameters. For example, $r=k-1$ is a supplied
formula, not a synthesized threshold. Region selection is evaluated anew
at each successor, so the verification conditions include transitions
between regions. Continuity across region boundaries is not required.

The constraints remain \eqref{eq:syn-positive}--\eqref{eq:syn-domination}.
The proof of \Cref{prop:synthesis-soundness} therefore applies verbatim.
With a fixed partition and fixed coefficient bound, the
candidate set remains finite. The learner and verifier queries remain
Boolean combinations of linear arithmetic under the preceding input
restrictions. Thus the same finite-template termination argument applies.
The partition itself is not synthesized, and additional regions can make
the search more expensive.

\subsection{Synthesis Results}
\label{sec:synthesis-examples}

\Cref{tab:synthesis-results} reports a run with Z3~5.1.0, using the same
kernel, objective, and coefficient schedule throughout. Limits were ten
seconds per solver call and 500 candidates per input. Each final obligation
was checked separately. Candidate counts depend on solver tie-breaking;
these runs are not a performance comparison with other tools.

\paragraph{Unbounded symmetric random walk.}
For the walk in \Cref{sec:walk}, $S\equiv x\geq0$ yields the automatic
basis $(x,1)$. Starting from the automatically selected sample $x=1$,
the recorded search first proposes $V=1$, $U=x$. The verifier returns
$x=2$, violating $U\leq V$. The next candidate is $V=U=x$, verified
for every integer $x>0$. The supermartingale is a martingale, while the
left outcome decreases $U$ by one. This example establishes AST despite
infinite expected runtime: for $0<x<N$, the expected time $x(N-x)$ to
hit either $0$ or $N$ lower-bounds the time to hit $0$, and diverges as
$N$ grows. Uniform negative expected drift is not required.

\paragraph{FDR with a symbolic range.}
For the loop of \Cref{sec:fdr}, the input supplies
\[
 S\equiv n\geq1\land1\leq v<2n\land0\leq c<v\land c<n
\]
and the complete updates, for $\beta\in\{0,1\}$,
\[
 T_\beta(n,v,c)=
 \begin{cases}
 (n,2v-n,2c+\beta-n),&2c+\beta\geq n,\\
 (n,2v,2c+\beta),&2c+\beta<n.
 \end{cases}
\]
The automatic basis is $(n,v,c,1)$, and the kernel finds $V=n$ and
$U=n-v+c$ on $v<n$, uniformly for symbolic $n$. Writing
$\Delta=v-c$, enabled states satisfy $1\leq\Delta\leq n-1$ and
$U=n-\Delta$. On bit zero, a continuing successor has
$\Delta'=2\Delta$, so $U'\leq U-1$; acceptance sets $U'=0$.
Since $n$ is unchanged, $V'$ is either $n$ or zero, and $U\leq V$.
The affine variant suffices for qualitative termination even though the
manual proof uses a logarithmic one. This instance remains a baseline:
on bit one, $\Delta'=2\Delta-1\geq\Delta$ when continuing, so
$\mathbb{E}[U']\leq U-1/2$. Thus this particular $U$ is already a
ranking supermartingale.

\paragraph{FLDR with fixed weights.}
For weights $(4,5)$, preprocessing fixes $m=9$, $k=4$, $n=2$, and
rejection weight $7$. The supplied iteration semantics describes the
exact finite tree, and $S$ contains its valid nodes. Only $c,d$ remain
integer state variables, giving the automatic basis $(c,d,1)$.
After excluding $B=1$, the kernel finds $V=U=c-2d+2$ at $B=2$.
An independent audit of all five enabled nodes confirms this certificate
(\Cref{app:artifact}). At some nodes the expectation is unchanged, so the
shared function is not a ranking supermartingale with uniform negative drift.

\paragraph{FLDR with symbolic parameters.}
To handle arbitrary input trees, we supply a structural transition
abstraction. All declared integer components $(m,k,n,c,d)$ enter the
templates; a Boolean flag $\mathit{active}$ represents the concrete guard.
The supplied invariant is
\[
\begin{gathered}
 m\geq1,\quad0\leq k\leq m,\quad1\leq n\leq m,\\
 0\leq c\leq k,\quad d\geq0,\quad
 \mathit{active}\Rightarrow c<k.
\end{gathered}
\]
Each bit outcome is classified as continuation, rejection, or acceptance.
Continuation advances to $(c+1,2d+\beta)$ and remains active, requiring
$c+1<k$. Rejection resets to $(0,0)$ and remains active. Acceptance
advances to $(c+1,2d+\beta)$ and becomes terminal. Parameters are
unchanged; both outcomes may not reject simultaneously. The verifier
checks all admissible type pairs and retains the update of $d$.

The FLDR preprocessing properties in \Cref{sec:fldr} justify this
abstraction: the tree has height at most $k$, and each level contains at
most one leaf of a given label, hence at most one rejecting leaf.
This connection is supplied mathematically, not synthesized or
universally machine-checked from the preprocessing code. The domain
intentionally overapproximates $k=\lceil\log_2m\rceil$; no logarithmic
arithmetic is passed to the solver. When $k=0$, the initial state is
terminal.

The full basis $(m,k,n,c,d,1)$ yields $V=k$, $U=k-c$ on active states.
The solver sets the other coefficients to zero without a prescribed
feature subset. Every successor has $V'$ equal to $k$ or zero. A
non-rejecting outcome decreases $U$ by at least one, and $1\leq U\leq V$.
Thus one pair is verified for unbounded parameters under the structural
contract. Relative to the manual choice $V=m$, using $V=k$ also makes
the sufficient domination condition immediate.

\paragraph{Role of the two functions and limits of the evidence.}
The depth variant $k-c$ can increase in expectation: for the fixed input
$(4,5)$, node $(c,d)=(2,0)$ has successors $(3,0)$ and $(0,0)$,
so $U=2$ but $\mathbb{E}[U']=5/2$. In the restricted basis $(k,c,1)$,
no single function can meet both roles on this instance: at fixed $k=4$,
write it as $W(c)=A+Dc$. Progress at the root requires $D<0$, whereas
at $(2,0)$ its expected change is $-D/2>0$. However, the automatically
found $c-2d+2$ shows that this obstruction disappears when $d$ is allowed.
The result therefore concerns a specified template class, not an intrinsic
need for distinct functions in FLDR.

\paragraph{A piecewise certificate on both sides of zero.}
As an additional expressiveness check, consider the symmetric random walk
on all of $\mathbb Z$, with guard $x\neq0$ and invariant $\mathit{true}$.
Any affine variant $U(x)=ax+b$ positive on every active state must have
$a=0$, since both tails are unbounded. Such a constant variant cannot
decrease at $x=2$, whose two successors remain active. Hence no single-affine
pair satisfies our constraints, irrespective of the coefficient bound.
This analytic obstruction is separate from the bounded synthesis failures.

With the supplied split $x<0$ versus $x\geq0$, the same kernel finds
$V=U=|x|$ at $B=1$. The expectation is unchanged on active states and one
of the two successors decreases the certificate by one. Verification is
over all integer states. This demonstrates additional expressiveness for
a single template over the supplied invariant; manually splitting the
proof into its two sign-invariant domains would also suffice.

\paragraph{Optional sampler partitions.}
No extra partition is needed for the FDR and FLDR results in
\Cref{tab:synthesis-results}. As regression checks, the extended artifact
also supplies the semantically motivated splits $2v<n$ versus $2v\geq n$
for FDR, and $c<k-1$ versus $c\geq k-1$ for FLDR. The former separates
states in which the next doubling cannot yet accept from states in which
acceptance or recycling is possible. The latter isolates the last active
depth, where a child must accept or reset. On the fixed input $(4,5)$,
the threshold is $k-1=3$. All three partitioned sampler inputs synthesize
verified certificates with the original successful bounds, respectively
$1,2,1$. In the recorded FDR run the two regions receive identical
expressions. These are feasibility checks, not evidence that the additional
partitions are necessary or optimal.

The synthesis contribution is a reproducible feasibility demonstration
for the program-level obligations. Supplied invariants and body semantics,
the abstract FLDR contract, the affine or fixed-piecewise-affine hypothesis
class, and $U\leq V$
remain substantive assumptions. The experiments neither establish
synthesis completeness for AST programs nor demonstrate superiority to
existing synthesis tools. Automatic extraction of body semantics, automatic
partition selection, richer templates, and broader comparisons remain future work.
