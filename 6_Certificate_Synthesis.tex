\section{Template-Based Certificate Synthesis}
\label{sec:certificate-synthesis}

Having applied the rule manually in \Cref{sec:walk,sec:case-studies}, we now
instantiate it with an SMT-based search for two certificates over loop-head
states. The current prototype takes a supplied
inductive state predicate and a manually encoded one-iteration semantics.
It synthesizes the coefficients of the certificates; invariant generation and
automatic translation of source programs are outside its current scope.

\subsection{Guarded Affine Templates and Constraints}
\label{sec:synthesis-constraints}

Consider a loop with guard $b$ over integer-valued variables
$\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$. Let $S$ be a supplied predicate containing
the initial states and preserved by every enabled body execution. For the
presented examples, the body has two equiprobable outcomes represented by
total, piecewise affine updates $T_0,T_1$. Both updates describe an entire
body execution, including its deterministic branches.

We use independent templates
\[
 V_{\mathbf a}(\mathbf{x})=
 \begin{cases}a_0+\sum_{i=1}^d a_i x_i,&b(\mathbf{x}),\\0,&\neg b(\mathbf{x}),\end{cases}
 \qquad
 U_{\mathbf c}(\mathbf{x})=
 \begin{cases}c_0+\sum_{i=1}^d c_i x_i,&b(\mathbf{x}),\\0,&\neg b(\mathbf{x}).\end{cases}
\]
Both functions have integer coefficients between $-2$ and $2$.
The prototype prefers a small sum of absolute coefficient values.
This preference guides the search and has no role in certificate validity.

For every $\mathbf{x}$ satisfying $S\land b$, we impose
\begin{align}
 V(\mathbf{x})&\geq 1,& U(\mathbf{x})&\geq 1,
 \label{eq:syn-positive}\\
 V(T_0(\mathbf{x}))+V(T_1(\mathbf{x}))&\leq 2V(\mathbf{x}),
 \label{eq:syn-supermartingale}\\
 U(T_0(\mathbf{x}))\leq U(\mathbf{x})-1
 &\quad\lor\quad U(T_1(\mathbf{x}))\leq U(\mathbf{x})-1,
 \label{eq:syn-progress}\\
 U(\mathbf{x})&\leq V(\mathbf{x}).
 \label{eq:syn-domination}
\end{align}
The last condition ensures that $U$ is bounded on sublevel sets of $V$.
It is stronger than the corresponding premise of the original rule.
Likewise, integer coefficients and
$V\geq1$ restrict the search space beyond the requirements of that rule.

\begin{proposition}[Soundness of the synthesis constraints]
Assume that $S$ contains the initial states and is preserved by both body
outcomes. Every template instantiation satisfying
\eqref{eq:syn-positive}--\eqref{eq:syn-domination} for all states in
$S\land b$ is an AST certificate with progress bound $\varepsilon=1/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The templates vanish off the guard. On enabled states, $V$ is positive and
$U$ is a positive integer. Equation~\eqref{eq:syn-supermartingale} is the
one-iteration supermartingale inequality. Equation~\eqref{eq:syn-progress}
ensures a strict decrease of $U$ on an outcome of probability $1/2$.
Finally, $U\leq V$ on $S$, including terminal states, implies that
$U\leq\lfloor r\rfloor$ whenever $V\leq r$.
The set of subdistributions supported in $S$ is a distributional invariant
for the loop and the allowed inputs. Hence \Cref{thm:program-rule} applies.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counterexample-Guided Search}
\label{sec:synthesis-cegis}

The learner instantiates the constraints on a finite set of enabled states
and selects candidate coefficients. The verifier fixes these coefficients
and asks whether there exists any integer state satisfying $S\land b$
that violates a constraint. A satisfying state is added to the learner's
sample set; an unsatisfiable query establishes all constraints throughout
the invariant domain. Unknown results and resource limits are treated as
inconclusive.

With the states fixed, the learner's constraints are linear in the
coefficients. With the coefficients fixed, the verifier's constraints are
Boolean combinations of linear arithmetic over states. Thus the procedure
avoids solving the original existential-universal formula directly.
The final verification places no bound on the program variables.
Initialization and preservation of $S$ are checked independently.

For a fixed finite coefficient domain, every counterexample excludes the
current candidate. In the absence of resource limits and with terminating
exact solvers, this gives a terminating search that is complete only for
the chosen finite template space. It does not establish completeness for
affine certificates with unrestricted coefficients or for AST programs.

\subsection{Worked Synthesis Examples}
\label{sec:synthesis-examples}

\paragraph{Unbounded symmetric random walk.}
We return to the integer-valued random walk of \Cref{sec:walk}, with
$S\equiv x\geq0$. The templates are $V(x)=ax+b$ and
$U(x)=cx+d$ on enabled states, and zero at $x=0$.
In the recorded run, the learner starts from $x=1$ and first proposes
$V=U=1$ on enabled states. The verifier returns $x=2$: both successors
remain enabled and keep $U=1$, violating progress.
The next candidate is $V(x)=U(x)=x$, recovering the certificates from
\Cref{sec:walk}. The verifier establishes all constraints for every
integer $x>0$, and initialization and invariant preservation are checked
independently. Thus the procedure discovers and validates the earlier
manual proof over the unbounded state space.

This example separates AST synthesis from finite-expected-runtime
arguments. For $0<x<N$, the expected time to hit either $0$ or $N$ is
$x(N-x)$, which lower-bounds the expected time to hit $0$. Letting
$N$ grow shows that the latter expectation is infinite for $x>0$.
The synthesized $V$ is a martingale, not a certificate of uniform expected
decrease. The separate probabilistic progress condition still holds.

\paragraph{Fast Dice Roller with symbolic range.}
Use the FDR loop of \Cref{sec:fdr}, with initial values $v=1,c=0$ and
symbolic integer parameter $n\geq1$. Its invariant domain is
\[
 S\equiv n\geq1\ \land\ 1\leq v<2n\ \land\ 0\leq c<v\ \land\ c<n.
\]
For bit $\beta\in\{0,1\}$, the complete body update is
\[
 T_\beta(n,v,c)=
 \begin{cases}
 (n,2v-n,2c+\beta-n),&2c+\beta\geq n,\\
 (n,2v,2c+\beta),&2c+\beta<n.
 \end{cases}
\]
Independent affine templates over $(n,v,c)$ yield the verified pair
\[
 V(n,v,c)=\begin{cases}n,&v<n,\\0,&v\geq n,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 U(n,v,c)=\begin{cases}n-v+c,&v<n,\\0,&v\geq n.\end{cases}
\]
The shared engine finds this pair after four candidates in the recorded
run. Both certificates are verified for all integer states in $S$, with
no fixed upper bound on $n$.

At an enabled state, let $d=v-c$. Then $1\leq d\leq n-1$, so
$1\leq U=n-d\leq n-1<V$. Since $n$ is unchanged, either successor
has $V$ equal to $n$ or zero, proving the supermartingale condition.
On the zero-bit outcome, the difference becomes $d'=2d$: subtracting
$n$ from both variables leaves their difference unchanged. If the
successor remains enabled, $U'=n-2d\leq U-1$; otherwise $U'=0<U$.
Thus progress holds with probability at least $1/2$, and $U\leq V$
establishes sublevel boundedness.

The affine variant therefore suffices for qualitative termination; the
logarithmic variant used in \Cref{thm:fdr} is not required for this
purpose. FDR is nevertheless a baseline: on the one-bit outcome,
$d'=2d-1\geq d$, or execution terminates, so $U'\leq U$.
Consequently $\mathbb{E}[U']\leq U-1/2$, and this particular $U$ is
itself a ranking supermartingale. Together, the two examples demonstrate
symbolic-parameter synthesis and AST with infinite expected runtime;
they do not yet establish an advantage over existing synthesis tools.
