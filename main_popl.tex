\begin{abstract}
    We present a Hoare-like verification framework for discrete probabilistic programs with \emph{nondeterministic} choices, applied to two non-trivial case studies: the Nim game and an error-tolerant variant of Lumbroso's Fast Dice Roller in which fair coin flips are replaced by adversarial choices biased by up to~$\epsilon$.
    Our framework extends inductive \emph{distributional loop invariants} from the purely probabilistic setting to programs that combine randomness with nondeterminism, supporting both \emph{existential} verification (some strategy achieves the goal) and \emph{universal} verification (a property holds under all strategies of a chosen class).
    At the core of our development is a fine-grained landscape of program-level strategies---ranging from memoryless deterministic to history-dependent randomized---and an explicit translation theorem showing that program-level strategies precisely correspond to strategies on the program's underlying Markov decision process.
    As a key intermediate result, we prove that \emph{loop-counter} strategies are as expressive as full-memory strategies, yielding a more compact and intuitive program-level strategy that retains the full expressive power of full-memory strategies.
    Using this framework, we verify a winning strategy and a strategy-universal zero-sum property for the Nim game, and prove that the nondeterministic Fast Dice Roller produces a~$\epsilon$-uniform output distribution under \emph{all} adversarial coin strategies.

    \keywords{Sampling algorithms \and Loop invariants \and Probabilistic programs \and Nondeterministic programs \and Program verification \and Hoare logic \and Markov decision processes \and Strategies}
\end{abstract}
