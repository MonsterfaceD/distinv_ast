\section{Detailed One-Iteration Calculations}
\label{app:calculations}

\paragraph{FDR.}
Starting in $\sigma$, doubling $v$ and sampling the bit produces the two Dirac states $\sigma[v/2v,c/2c]$ and $\sigma[v/2v,c/2c+1]$, each of mass $1/2$. Filtering by $c\ge n$ and applying both subtraction assignments yields \Cref{eq:fdr-post}. On the left-bit outcome, subtraction of $n$ from both variables preserves their difference, hence in all enabled cases
\[
 (v'-c')=2(v-c).
\]
This is the algebraic core of the FDR variant argument.

\paragraph{FLDR.}
After incrementing $c$, the fair choice reaches indices $2d$ and $2d+1$. A rejecting child maps to $(c,d)=(0,0)$; a non-rejecting child retains depth $c+1$. Each level contains at most one leaf with the rejecting label, so the two children of an internal node cannot both reject. The total mass of non-reset successors is therefore at least $1/2$. A continuing successor reduces $k-c$ by exactly one; an accepting successor sets the guarded variant to zero, decreasing it by at least one. Rejection may instead increase the variant by resetting its value to $k$. These are the three outcome types used in the structural synthesis input of \Cref{sec:synthesis-examples}.
