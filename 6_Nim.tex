\section{Warm-Up: The One-Dimensional Random Walk}
\label{sec:walk}

Consider the symmetric random walk
\[
 \ass{x}{k};\quad
 \while{x>0}{\pchoice{\ass{x}{x-1}}{1/2}{\ass{x}{x+1}}},
 \qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.
\]
This loop terminates almost surely at $x=0$, although its expected termination time is infinite. It is therefore a compact illustration of why the supermartingale and variant play different roles.

Let $M=\{\dirac{\sigma}\mid\sigma(x)=k,\ k>0\}$ and
$I=\{\mu\in\subdists\mid\supp(\mu)\subseteq\{\sigma\mid\sigma(x)\geq0\}\}$.
The set $I$ is distributionally inductive. Choose
\[
 v(\sigma)=u(\sigma)=\max\{\sigma(x),0\}.
\]
For every enabled state,
\[
 \sum_{\sigma'}\post{B}(\dirac{\sigma})(\sigma')v(\sigma')
 =\tfrac12(x-1)+\tfrac12(x+1)=x=v(\sigma),
\]
so $v$ is in fact a martingale. With probability $1/2$, the left branch is taken and $u$ decreases by one; hence the decrease obligation holds with $\varepsilon=1/2$. Finally, on $v_{\le r}$ we have $u\le r$. \Cref{thm:program-rule} proves AST.

The same mathematical idea can be expressed at pCFG level, but the certificate becomes location-dependent. A loop iteration contains the guard, a probabilistic choice, and an assignment. A pCFG variant therefore uses a scaling factor (three in a direct translation) and different additive offsets at the intermediate locations to force strict descent across every deterministic edge. The supermartingale similarly anticipates the next assignment at branch locations. None of this bookkeeping represents probabilistic insight; it is introduced by the operational granularity. The program rule preserves the natural proof: ``distance $x$ is a martingale and decreases with probability one half.''

A walled walk with $0\le x\le W$ admits an even older bounded-variant argument~\cite{DBLP:series/mcs/McIverM05}. Removing the wall destroys global boundedness but not AST. Sublevel-set boundedness is precisely what recovers the unbounded example and previews the sampler proofs below.

The same construction extends to the standard two-dimensional walk when termination is phrased as hitting an appropriate absorbing boundary and a suitable radial or coordinate certificate is available. We keep the one-dimensional version because it isolates the proof rule without geometric side conditions; the substantial case studies concern sampling algorithms.
