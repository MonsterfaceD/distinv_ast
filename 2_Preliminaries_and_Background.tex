\section{Preliminaries and Background}
\label{sec:prelims}

Fix a finite set $\vars$ of variables and the countable state space
$\Sigma=\mathbb Q^{\vars}$. Expressions denote total functions on states;
updates preserve $\Sigma$. For a Boolean expression $b$, write
$\llbracket b\rrbracket=\{\sigma\mid\sigma\models b\}$ and $[b]$ for its
Iverson bracket. The update of $x$ to $a(\sigma)$ is
$\sigma[x/a(\sigma)]$. We use $\mathbb N=\{0,1,\ldots\}$.

\subsection{Programs, Subdistributions, and AST}

Our probabilistic guarded-command language is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C &::= \ski\mid\ass{x}{a}\mid\comp{C}{C}\mid\pchoice{C}{p}{C}\\
   &\phantom{{}::={}}\mid\ifelse{b}{C}{C}\mid\while{b}{C}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
where each $p\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q$ is constant. There is no
nondeterministic choice. A \emph{subdistribution} is a function
$\mu:\Sigma\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ with weight
$\weight{\mu}=\sum_\sigma\mu(\sigma)\leq1$. Write $\subdists$ for their
set, $\supp(\mu)=\{\sigma\mid\mu(\sigma)>0\}$, and $\dirac{\sigma}$
for a Dirac distribution. For a set $M$ of subdistributions, put
$\supp(M)=\bigcup_{\mu\in M}\supp(\mu)$.

A program denotes a transformer $\post{C}:\subdists\to\subdists$.
It is linear, $\omega$-continuous, and does not increase mass. The clauses
follow the forward semantics of Kozen~\cite{DBLP:conf/focs/Kozen79,DBLP:conf/stoc/Kozen83}:
\begin{align*}
 \post{\ski}(\mu)&=\mu,\\
 \post{\ass{x}{a}}(\mu)(\sigma)
   &=\sum_{\sigma':\,\sigma'[x/a(\sigma')]=\sigma}\mu(\sigma'),\\
 \post{\comp{C_1}{C_2}}(\mu)
   &=\post{C_2}(\post{C_1}(\mu)),\\
 \post{\pchoice{C_1}{p}{C_2}}(\mu)
   &=p\post{C_1}(\mu)+(1-p)\post{C_2}(\mu),\\
 \post{\ifelse{b}{C_1}{C_2}}(\mu)
   &=\post{C_1}([b]\mu)+\post{C_2}([\neg b]\mu).
\end{align*}
For a loop, take a least fixed point over transformers, as
in~\cite{zilken2026verifyingsamplingalgorithmsdistributional}. Let
$\mathcal H$ be the pointed $\omega$-cpo of $\omega$-continuous,
mass-nonincreasing maps $\subdists\to\subdists$, ordered pointwise, with
bottom $\bot=\lambda\eta.0$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_{b,B}(f)(\eta)&=[\neg b]\eta+f\bigl(\post{B}([b]\eta)\bigr),\\
 \post{\while{b}{B}}(\mu)
   &=(\lfp\Phi_{b,B})(\mu)
     =\sup_{j\in\mathbb N}\Phi_{b,B}^j(\bot)(\mu).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:while-transformer-fixpoint}
\end{equation}
The functional is $\omega$-continuous and preserves $\mathcal H$
(\Cref{app:translation}). Its $j$th approximant collects executions
terminating after fewer than $j$ body iterations. Thus missing mass
records nontermination. Linearity and continuity imply
\[
 \post{C}\Bigl(\sum_i p_i\dirac{\sigma_i}\Bigr)
   =\sum_i p_i\post{C}(\dirac{\sigma_i})
 \qquad(p_i\geq0,\ \textstyle\sum_i p_i\leq1),
\]
including countably many summands. This permits reduction to Dirac inputs.

\begin{definition}[Almost-sure termination]
A program $C$ is \emph{almost-surely terminating} on $M\subseteq\subdists$
if $\weight{\post{C}(\mu)}=\weight{\mu}$ for every $\mu\in M$.
\end{definition}
AST does not require finite expected runtime.

\subsection{Distributional Invariants and Certificates}

\begin{definition}[Distributional invariant]
For $C=\while{b}{B}$ and initial distributions $M$, a set
$I\subseteq\subdists$ is a \emph{distributional invariant} if
\[
 M\subseteq I
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 [\neg b]\mu+\post{B}([b]\mu)\in I\quad\text{for all }\mu\in I.
\]
\end{definition}
The invariant retains mass that has already terminated and advances the
enabled mass by one body execution. Besides state bounds it can express
relations between probabilities, such as uniformity within a decision-tree
row~\cite{zilken2026verifyingsamplingalgorithmsdistributional}.
The AST rule uses its support $S=\supp(I)$; the stronger distributional
information remains available for partial correctness.

The rule employs two state functions. A nonnegative \emph{supermartingale}
$v:S\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ does not increase in expectation over an enabled
body execution. Its sublevel sets are
$v_{\leq r}=\{\sigma\in S\mid v(\sigma)\leq r\}$.
A \emph{variant} $u:S\to\mathbb N$ decreases with uniformly positive
probability and is bounded on each such sublevel set. It need not decrease
in expectation: a rejected sample may reset $u$ upwards. All certificate
sums below range over positive-mass outcomes. Support closure, proved in
\Cref{sec:metatheory}, ensures that the functions are defined there.

\subsection{The pCFG Rule and Operational Correspondence}
\label{sec:background}

Majumdar and Sathiyanarayana~\cite{DBLP:journals/pacmpl/MajumdarS25}
give a sound and relatively complete AST rule for probabilistic
control-flow graphs (pCFGs), including bounded demonic nondeterminism.
We use its purely probabilistic specialization with fixed probabilities.
A configuration $\gamma=(\ell,\sigma)$ comprises a control location and
a valuation. Assignment and conditional locations have one enabled
successor; probabilistic locations choose among finitely many successors.
The program exit $\ell_{\mathsf{out}}$ is absorbing. Write
$\Succ(\gamma)$ for the positive-probability successors and
$P(\gamma,\gamma')$ for their transition probabilities, with $P=1$ at
deterministic steps. An invariant $\mathit{Inv}$ for initial valuations
$E$ contains all $(\ell_{\mathsf{init}},\sigma)$ with $\sigma\in E$ and
is closed under these transitions.

\begin{theorem}[pCFG AST rule, adapted from~\cite{DBLP:journals/pacmpl/MajumdarS25}]
\label{thm:pcfg-rule}
A pCFG is AST from $E$ if an inductive invariant $\mathit{Inv}$ admits
$V:\mathit{Inv}\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}$ and
$U:\mathit{Inv}\to\mathbb N$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $V(\gamma)=U(\gamma)=0$ at terminal configurations, and
       $V(\gamma)>0$ at every other configuration;
 \item for every nonterminal $\gamma\in\mathit{Inv}$,
 \[
   \sum_{\gamma'\in\Succ(\gamma)}P(\gamma,\gamma')V(\gamma')
   \leq V(\gamma);
 \]
 \item for every $r\geq0$, the values of $U$ on
 $V_{\leq r}=\{\gamma\in\mathit{Inv}\mid V(\gamma)\leq r\}$ are
 bounded, and some $\varepsilon_r>0$ satisfies
 \[
   \sum_{\substack{\gamma'\in\Succ(\gamma)\\U(\gamma')<U(\gamma)}}
      P(\gamma,\gamma')\geq\varepsilon_r
 \]
 for every nonterminal $\gamma\in V_{\leq r}$.
\end{enumerate}
Conversely, AST from $E$ implies the existence of these certificates.
Under the arithmetic assumptions of \Cref{sec:metatheory}, they are
representable in the assertion language.
\end{theorem}
At a deterministic step the last condition forces strict descent of
$U$, so this unified notation includes the usual separate condition for
assignments and guards. It also forces $U>0$ away from the exit.
Soundness combines bounded-variant reasoning within each sublevel set
with the nonnegative supermartingale bound on escape. For completeness,
\Cref{app:initial-sets} adapts the published construction from a fixed
initial configuration to a common pair of certificates for arbitrary
initial sets $E$.

The structural translation $\mathcal T[C]$ turns assignments into update
edges, choices into branching locations, and the loop guard into a
conditional location whose true successor enters the body. Sequential
composition identifies adjacent exits and entries, and the body exit is
identified with the loop guard. For a loop-free body, structural induction
shows that $\post{B}(\dirac{\sigma})(\sigma')$ equals the total probability
of body paths from entry valuation $\sigma$ to exit valuation $\sigma'$.
Loop unrolling then gives
\[
 C\text{ is AST on }M
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal T[C]\text{ is AST from }\supp(M).
\]
\Cref{app:translation} proves both facts. The program-level rule contracts
these finite body paths into one semantic step; certificates need only
annotate the valuations at loop entry.
