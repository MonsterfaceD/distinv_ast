\section{Case Studies: Exact Sampling from Fair Bits}
\label{sec:case-studies}

\subsection{Fast Dice Roller: the same proof at two levels}
\label{sec:fdr}

The Fast Dice Roller (FDR) samples uniformly from $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ using fair coin flips~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1304-1916}. We use the following program-level presentation:
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,mathescape=true,frame=single]
$v := 1;\ c := 0;$
while ($v<n$) do
  $v := 2v;$
  $c := 2c$ [1/2] $c := 2c+1;$
  if ($c\ge n$) then $v:=v-n;\ c:=c-n$ else skip
end
\end{lstlisting}
On termination, $c$ is the sampled outcome. Existing distributional-invariant reasoning proves that the terminating output is uniform~\cite{zilken2026verifyingsamplingalgorithmsdistributional}; AST completes total correctness. The distributional invariant taken from ~\cite{zilken2026verifyingsamplingalgorithmsdistributional} states that at the loop head, the reachable states satisfy
\begin{equation}
 0\le c<\min(v,n),\qquad 0<v<2n,\qquad n>0.
 \label{eq:fdr-support}
\end{equation}
The distributional invariant additionally states that equal admissible $c$-values carry equal mass, however only \eqref{eq:fdr-support} is needed for our termination proof.

Let $B_{\mathrm{FDR}}$ denote the body. For a Dirac input $\dirac{\sigma}$, its post-distribution is
\begin{align}
\post{B_{\mathrm{FDR}}}(\dirac{\sigma})
={}&\tfrac12[c<n]\bigl(
 \dirac{\sigma[v/2v,c/2c]}+\dirac{\sigma[v/2v,c/2c+1]}\bigr)\notag\\
&+\tfrac12[c\geq0]\bigl(
 \dirac{\sigma[v/2v-n,c/2c-n]}+
 \dirac{\sigma[v/2v-n,c/2c+1-n]}\bigr),
\label{eq:fdr-post}
\end{align}
where brackets are evaluated after the corresponding updates. This compact expression is the only operational calculation required by the program-level proof.

\begin{theorem}[AST of FDR]
\label{thm:fdr}
For every $n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$, FDR terminates almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the existing distributional invariant and define
\[
 v(\sigma)=
 \begin{cases}0&v\ge n,\\ n&v<n,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 u(\sigma)=
 \begin{cases}
 0&v\ge n,\\
 \left\lceil\log_2\!\left(\dfrac{n}{v-c}\right)\right\rceil&v<n.
 \end{cases}
\]
The supermartingale obligation is immediate: $n$ is unchanged and every successor is assigned either $n$ or $0$. For progress, inspect the outcome generated by the left fair branch. Before possible subtraction of $n$, both $v$ and $c$ are doubled; after subtraction their difference remains $2(v-c)$. Hence in either case the denominator of $n/(v-c)$ doubles, so $u$ strictly decreases. The left bit has probability $1/2$, giving $\varepsilon=1/2$.

For a sublevel set with $r<n$, only terminal states occur. If $r\ge n$, the invariant bounds $n\le r$, $0<v<2n$, and $0\le c<v$, so $v-c\ge1$ and $u\le\lceil\log_2 r\rceil$. All obligations of \Cref{thm:program-rule} hold.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comparison with the pCFG proof.}
The pCFG rule of Majumdar and Sathiyanarayana is applicable to FDR and
establishes the same AST statement as \Cref{thm:fdr}.  Unlike the
program-level proof, however, it reasons about individual configurations
$(\ell,\sigma)$ of the graph in \Cref{fig:fdr-pcfg}.  Thus, the initialisation,
loop guard, doubling of $v$, fair-bit choice, conditional, and the two
subtraction assignments all become separate proof obligations.

For completeness, we spell out the corresponding pCFG certificate.  Write
$\mathit{Inv}_\ell$ for the states admitted at location $\ell$.  The invariant
is obtained by transporting the support information
\eqref{eq:fdr-support} through the intermediate assignments:
\begin{align*}
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_{\mathsf{init}}}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_1}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
                    \land \sigma(v)=1\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_2}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
                    \land 1\leq\sigma(v)<2\sigma(n)
                    \land 0\leq\sigma(c)<\min\{\sigma(v),\sigma(n)\}\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_3}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma\in\mathit{Inv}_{\ell_2}\mid
                    \sigma(v)<\sigma(n)\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_4}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
                    \land 2\leq\sigma(v)\leq2\sigma(n)-2
                    \land 0\leq\sigma(c)<\tfrac{\sigma(v)}2\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_{5a}}
   &\coloneqq \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_4},
 \qquad
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_{5b}}
   \coloneqq \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_4},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_6}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
                    \land 2\leq\sigma(v)\leq2\sigma(n)-2
                    \land 0\leq\sigma(c)<\sigma(v)\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_7}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma\in\mathit{Inv}_{\ell_6}\mid
                   \sigma(c)\geq\sigma(n)\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_8}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma \mid \sigma(n)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
                    \land 1\leq\sigma(v)\leq\sigma(n)-2
                    \land \sigma(n)\leq\sigma(c)<\sigma(v)+\sigma(n)\},\\
 \mathit{Inv}_{\ell_{\mathsf{out}}}
   &\coloneqq \{\sigma\in\mathit{Inv}_{\ell_2}\mid
                    \sigma(v)\geq\sigma(n)\}.
\end{align*}
Hence
\[
    \mathit{Inv}
    \coloneqq
    \bigcup_{\ell\in\{\ell_{\mathsf{init}},\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_8,
                        \ell_{\mathsf{out}}\}}
    \bigl(\{\ell\}\times\mathit{Inv}_{\ell}\bigr)
\]
is an inductive pCFG invariant.  The loop-head component
$\mathit{Inv}_{\ell_2}$ is precisely the support part of the distributional
invariant reused in the program-level proof; the other components are
location-specific consequences of it.

The supermartingale is simple but already location-dependent through the
terminal location:
\[
    V(\ell,\sigma)
    \coloneqq
    \begin{cases}
        0, & \ell=\ell_{\mathsf{out}},\\
        \sigma(n), & \ell\neq\ell_{\mathsf{out}}.
    \end{cases}
\]
Since $n$ is never modified, every nonterminal deterministic successor has
the same $V$-value as its predecessor, while a transition to
$\ell_{\mathsf{out}}$ decreases it to $0$.  At the sole probabilistic location
$\ell_4$, both successors retain the value $\sigma(n)$.  Thus the
supermartingale obligations are immediate.  Moreover, every sublevel set
$V_{\leq r}$ fixes $n\leq r$; together with the bounds in
$\mathit{Inv}$ this makes all non-initial parts of the sublevel set finite.

The pCFG variant must instead account for the exact point within an iteration.
One suitable choice is the following location-specific refinement of the
iteration-level distance
$\left\lceil\log_2\!\bigl(n/(v-c)\bigr)\right\rceil$:
\[
U(\ell,\sigma)\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
  7\left\lceil\log_2(\sigma(n))\right\rceil+3,
    & \ell=\ell_{\mathsf{init}},\\[2pt]
  7\left\lceil\log_2(\sigma(n))\right\rceil+2,
    & \ell=\ell_1,\\[2pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(\dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-\sigma(c)}\right)
    \right\rceil+1,
    & \ell=\ell_2,\\[8pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(\dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-\sigma(c)}\right)
    \right\rceil,
    & \ell=\ell_3,\\[8pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(
        \dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\frac{\sigma(v)}2-\sigma(c)}
      \right)
    \right\rceil-1,
    & \ell=\ell_4,\\[10pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(
        \dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-2\sigma(c)}
      \right)
    \right\rceil+5,
    & \ell=\ell_{5a},\\[10pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(
        \dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-(2\sigma(c)+1)}
      \right)
    \right\rceil+5,
    & \ell=\ell_{5b},\\[10pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(\dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-\sigma(c)}\right)
    \right\rceil+4,
    & \ell=\ell_6,\\[10pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(\dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)-\sigma(c)}\right)
    \right\rceil+3,
    & \ell=\ell_7,\\[10pt]
  7\left\lceil
      \log_2\!\left(
        \dfrac{\sigma(n)}{\sigma(v)+\sigma(n)-\sigma(c)}
      \right)
    \right\rceil+2,
    & \ell=\ell_8,\\[10pt]
  0,
    & \ell=\ell_{\mathsf{out}}.
\end{cases}
\]

The factor $7$ bounds the number of pCFG transitions needed for one source-level
loop iteration.  The additive offsets ensure strict descent on transitions
that merely change the control location.  The expressions at
$\ell_4,\ell_{5a},\ell_{5b}$ additionally compensate for the fact that one of
the two updates of the iteration has already been executed.  For example,
at $\ell_4$ the current value of $v$ has already been doubled whereas $c$
still has its old value, explaining the denominator
$\sigma(v)/2-\sigma(c)$.  At $\ell_{5a}$ and $\ell_{5b}$, the expressions anticipate the
respective updates of $c$, which are executed on the outgoing edges.
The deterministic decrease conditions can consequently be discharged by
checking the edges of \Cref{fig:fdr-pcfg}; the guard edge to
$\ell_{\mathsf{out}}$ decreases to $0$, and the offsets enforce strict
decrease on the remaining deterministic edges.  At $\ell_4$, the
$\ell_{5a}$ successor---corresponding to the fair-bit outcome $0$---has a
strictly smaller variant.  This successor has probability $\frac12$.
Semantically, this is the familiar iteration-level observation that choosing
the zero bit doubles $v-c$; the rejection updates subtract $n$ from both
variables and hence preserve that difference.  In the pCFG proof this single
observation is distributed over the locations
$\ell_4,\ell_{5a},\ell_6,\ell_7,\ell_8$.

Finally, $U$ is bounded in every sublevel set $V_{\leq r}$.  Indeed,
$V\leq r$ implies $\sigma(n)\leq r$, and the invariant bounds all relevant
values of $v$ and $c$; the two initialization locations are harmless because
their variant values depend only on $n$.  Thus the pCFG rule applies with
$\varepsilon_r=\frac12$ for every $r$.

In comparison, the program-level proof uses one support invariant, the two
state functions displayed in \Cref{thm:fdr}, and the single body calculation
\eqref{eq:fdr-post}.  It also directly reuses the invariant required for the
uniform-output proof.  Both proof routes have the same proving power, but the
program-level rule expresses the algorithmic fact---progress over a complete
loop iteration---without introducing locations, offsets, and intermediate
state expressions solely for control-flow bookkeeping.


\subsection{Fast Loaded Dice Roller: rejection without graph bookkeeping}
\label{sec:fldr}

The Fast Loaded Dice Roller (FLDR) samples outcome $i$ with probability $a_i/m$, where $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ are positive integers and $m=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i$~\cite{DBLP:conf/aistats/SaadFRM20}. Let $k=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$. Preprocessing adds a rejecting outcome $n+1$ of weight $2^k-m$ and builds a compact representation $(H,h)$ of a binary decision tree. Sampling traverses the tree using fair bits; reaching $n+1$ resets the traversal to the root.

Abstracting the terminating preprocessing, the sampling loop is:
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,mathescape=true,frame=single]
while ($d < \phi_h(c)$) do
  $c := c+1;$
  $d := 2d$ [1/2] $d := 2d+1;$
  if ($H[d,c]=n+1$) then $c:=0;\ d:=0$ else skip
end
\end{lstlisting}
Here $c$ is the current depth and $d$ the node index; $\phi_h(c)$ identifies the first leaf position in row $c$. The preprocessing guarantees two facts used below: the tree height is at most $k$, and no internal node has both children equal to the rejecting leaf. Equivalently, from every enabled state at least one fair-bit successor avoids reset and advances one level towards a valid leaf.

The distributional invariant from the partial-correctness proof bounds reachable states by
\begin{equation}
 0\le c\le k,\qquad 0\le d\le n,
 \label{eq:fldr-support}
\end{equation}
and additionally records equal-mass and output-probability constraints. Again, the latter are retained for total correctness but are not consumed by AST.

\begin{theorem}[AST of FLDR]
\label{thm:fldr}
For every finite vector of positive integer weights, the FLDR sampling loop terminates almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $b$ be the loop guard. Define
\[
 v(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\sigma\not\models b,\\m&\sigma\models b,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 u(\sigma)=\begin{cases}0&\sigma\not\models b,\\k-c&\sigma\models b.\end{cases}
\]
Neither the weights nor $m$ are modified. Every post-state therefore has $v$ equal to $m$ if the loop remains enabled and $0$ otherwise, proving the supermartingale inequality.

Consider an enabled node. Each fair-bit outcome first increments $c$. If it is not the rejecting leaf, no reset occurs and $u$ decreases by one. The preprocessing property excludes the case in which both children reject. Therefore at least one of the two outcomes has smaller $u$, and its probability is $1/2$. The decrease condition holds with $\varepsilon=1/2$ even though the other outcome may reset $(c,d)$ and increase $u$ back to $k$.

Finally, on $v_{\le r}$, either $r<m$ and only terminal states occur, or $m\le r$. In the latter case $k=\lceil\log_2m\rceil\le\lceil\log_2r\rceil$, while \eqref{eq:fldr-support} gives $0\le u=k-c\le k$. Thus $u$ is bounded in every sublevel set, and \Cref{thm:program-rule} applies.
\end{proof}

This proof isolates the actual AST argument in three facts: $m$ is stable, tree height is logarithmically bounded by $m$, and at least one child avoids rejection. A direct pCFG treatment would additionally require values at the increment, choice, test, and reset locations, plus cases for both children. FLDR complements the FDR comparison by showing how the program-level rule accommodates rejection and restart through the post-distribution of one iteration.

\paragraph{From AST to total correctness.}
The reused distributional invariants establish that the terminating FDR output is uniform and that FLDR returns $i$ with probability $a_i/m$~\cite{zilken2026verifyingsamplingalgorithmsdistributional}. \Cref{thm:fdr,thm:fldr} show AST, i.e. that the corresponding post-distributions have total mass one. Hence the partial-correctness distributions are proper output distributions, yielding total correctness for both algorithms.



\subsection{Han--Hoshi Sampling: dyadic refinement without tail bounds}
\label{sec:HanHoshi}

The Han--Hoshi sampler~\cite{HanHoshi1997} draws from a discrete distribution with positive
integer weights $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and total weight
$m=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i$.  Write $S_i=\sum_{j=1}^i a_j$, with $S_0=0$.
The algorithm repeatedly refines a dyadic interval until it lies entirely
inside one of the target intervals
\[
 I_i=\left[\frac{S_{i-1}}m,\frac{S_i}m\right),
 \qquad 1\le i\le n.
\]
Its sampling loop is:
\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,mathescape=true,frame=single]
$v := 1;\ c := 0;\ r := 0;$
while ($r=0$) do
  $v := 2v;$
  $c := 2c$ [1/2] $c := 2c+1;$
  $r := \idx(c,v)$
end
\end{lstlisting}
Here
\[
 \idx(c,v)
 \coloneqq
 \begin{cases}
 i,
 & \text{if }S_{i-1}v\le cm\text{ and }(c+1)m\le S_iv
   \text{ for some }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\\
 0,&\text{if no such }i\text{ exists}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The pair $(c,v)$ represents the interval
\[
 J(c,v)=\left[\frac cv,\frac{c+1}v\right).
\]
At loop heads the reachable states satisfy
\begin{equation}
 v>0,\qquad 0\le c<v,\qquad 0\le r\le n.
 \label{eq:han-hoshi-support}
\end{equation}
Moreover, after an iteration, $r=0$ exactly when the selected child
interval is not contained in a target interval.  As in the preceding case
studies, a stronger distributional invariant used for output correctness
may be retained; only the support information above is needed for AST.

Let $B_{\mathrm{HH}}$ denote the loop body.  For $b\in\{0,1\}$, set
\[
 c_b=2c+b,\qquad v'=2v,\qquad r_b=\idx(c_b,v').
\]
For a Dirac input $\dirac{\sigma}$,
\begin{equation}
 \post{B_{\mathrm{HH}}}(\dirac{\sigma})
 =\tfrac12\dirac{\sigma[v/v',c/c_0,r/r_0]}
 +\tfrac12\dirac{\sigma[v/v',c/c_1,r/r_1]}.
 \label{eq:han-hoshi-post}
\end{equation}
Thus one complete program-level step is precisely a fair choice between
the left and right halves of $J(c,v)$.

\begin{theorem}[AST of Han--Hoshi]
 \label{thm:han-hoshi}
For every finite vector of positive integer weights, the Han--Hoshi
sampling loop terminates almost surely.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a reachable state $\sigma$, let
\[
 N(\sigma)
 =\left|\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}\,\middle|\,
 \frac cv<\frac{S_i}{m}<\frac{c+1}{v}\right\}\right|
\]
be the number of internal target boundaries lying strictly inside
$J(c,v)$.  Define the two certificates
\[
 \mathsf V(\sigma)=\mathsf U(\sigma)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&r\ne0,\\
 \max\{1,N(\sigma)\},&r=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
They vanish exactly on terminal states, and $\mathsf U$ is
natural-valued.

Consider an enabled state and write $q=N(\sigma)$.  Splitting $J(c,v)$
into its two children cannot create an internal target boundary.  Every
boundary counted in a child is counted in the parent, while a boundary at
the common endpoint of the children is counted in neither.  If $N_b$
denotes the number of boundaries strictly inside child $b$, then
\begin{equation}
 N_0+N_1\le q.
 \label{eq:han-hoshi-boundary-split}
\end{equation}
A child remains enabled exactly when it contains an internal boundary in
its interior; hence its post-state certificate is $N_b$ if $N_b>0$ and
zero otherwise.  Using \eqref{eq:han-hoshi-post} and
\eqref{eq:han-hoshi-boundary-split}, for $q>0$ we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma[\mathsf V']
 =\tfrac12(N_0+N_1)
 \le\tfrac q2
 \le \mathsf V(\sigma).
\]
If $q=0$, both children are terminal and the expectation is zero.  This
proves the supermartingale obligation of \Cref{thm:program-rule}.

For the decrease obligation, if $q>0$, at least one of $N_0,N_1$ is
strictly smaller than $q$: otherwise their sum would be at least $2q$,
contradicting \eqref{eq:han-hoshi-boundary-split}.  The corresponding
post-state therefore has strictly smaller $\mathsf U$.  It is selected
with probability $1/2$, so the decrease condition holds with
$\varepsilon=1/2$.  If $q=0$, both children terminate and decrease
$\mathsf U$ from $1$ to $0$, so the same bound applies.

Finally, $\mathsf U=\mathsf V$ on every reachable state.  Consequently,
on each sublevel set $\mathsf V_{\le R}$ we have
$\mathsf U\le R$.  All obligations of \Cref{thm:program-rule} hold, and
the loop is AST.
\end{proof}

The proof replaces the explicit depth-$t$ tail calculation by one
iteration-level fact: bisecting an interval distributes its internal
target boundaries over the two children, and at least one child contains
strictly fewer.  The number of such boundaries serves simultaneously as
the supermartingale and the natural-valued variant (with the value $1$
for the enabled boundary-free initial case).  Hence Han--Hoshi supplies a further application of the
program-level rule of \Cref{thm:program-rule}, with a certificate based on
interval refinement rather than the progress-and-restart structure of
FDR and FLDR.
