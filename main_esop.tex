\begin{abstract}
Almost-sure termination (AST) is essential to the correctness of exact
samplers, but rejection and restart complicate progress arguments.
We formulate a program-level version of the two-certificate AST rule of
Majumdar and Sathiyanarayana for probabilistic while-loops with loop-free
bodies and fixed choice probabilities. A natural-valued variant provides
probabilistic progress; a non-negative supermartingale controls escape
from regions where the variant is bounded. Both certificates concern
complete iterations in distribution-transformer semantics. We prove
soundness and relative completeness through translations to and from
pCFG certificates, without requiring users to annotate internal locations.

We give proofs for the Fast Dice Roller (FDR), Fast Loaded Dice Roller
(FLDR), and Han--Hoshi sampler, and compare FDR annotations at both levels.
A shared counterexample-guided synthesis kernel searches for two guarded
affine or fixed-piecewise-affine certificates from supplied invariants and
iteration semantics. Reproduced instances include the unbounded random
walk, symbolic FDR, a concrete FLDR tree, and a structural FLDR abstraction.
Final SMT queries verify the certificate constraints throughout the input
domains. The search is restricted by bounded integer coefficients and a
sufficient domination condition; abstraction soundness remains a separate
mathematical obligation. These examples demonstrate feasibility, including
AST with infinite expected runtime, rather than a general performance
advantage or synthesis completeness for AST programs.

\keywords{almost-sure termination \and probabilistic programs
\and sampling algorithms \and distribution transformers
\and supermartingales \and template-based synthesis}
\end{abstract}
